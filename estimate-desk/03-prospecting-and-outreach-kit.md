# Prospecting & Outreach Kit

## 1. Build the list (target 40 businesses in ONE metro)
Pick one mid-size metro within a 1-hour drive of you (you may need to meet owners). Sources, all public:
1. Your **state contractor-licensing board's public lookup** (confirms the business is real and licensed).
2. **Google Maps** search "HVAC repair [city]" — record business name, website, public phone, owner name if listed.
3. The business's own website "About/Contact" page.

**Qualify (record yes/no in the CSV):** 3–15 techs (look at team page/photos), uses online scheduling or a Jobber/Housecall Pro/ServiceTitan booking widget, active reviews in last 60 days (still operating), residential focus. **Verify every phone/email on the business's own site; leave blank rather than guess.** Skip national franchises.

## 2. Contact lawfully (not legal advice; confirm with an attorney)
From general knowledge; I could not open the FTC/FCC pages in this session, so verify each point.
- **Phone:** Call published *business* lines yourself (manual dial, no autodialer or prerecorded voice). Between 8am–9pm in the recipient's local time; some states are stricter. Keep an internal do-not-call list and honor "don't call" immediately. Business-to-business calls are largely outside the National Do Not Call Registry, but numbers that are an owner's personal cell can be treated as residential — don't call those.
- **Text:** Don't cold-text prospects. Marketing texts to mobile numbers generally need prior express written consent, and several states add their own rules.
- **Email:** CAN-SPAM: truthful sender/subject, your physical mailing address, a working opt-out honored within 10 business days. Send one-to-one, personalized messages, never purchased lists.
- **Recording:** Don't record calls unless you announce it; some states require all-party consent.
- **Delivering the service:** the contractor's customers must be contacted under the contractor's authority and consent. Phase 1 uses live calls and email only; add texting only after the contractor shows consent records. Keep an opt-out list per client.
- Be honest about who you are and why you're calling. State laws on telemarketing registration vary — check your state before volume outreach.

## 3. Cold email (one-to-one, ~90 words)
> Subject: Unsold estimates at [Company]
>
> Hi [First name], I found [Company] while researching HVAC shops in [city] — your [specific detail, e.g. recent review about a same-day install] stood out.
>
> I run a small service that follows up with homeowners who got an estimate but haven't decided. Most owners I'd expect to be too busy to chase them.
>
> Could I look at how your open estimates are handled today? No pitch deck; 15 minutes. If it's not relevant, tell me and I won't write again.
>
> [Name] · [Company/Brand] · [Mailing address]

## 4. Call script (≈60 seconds before the question)
1. "Hi, [Name]? I'm [You] — I'm not a customer, this is a cold call, so give me 20 seconds and tell me if it's a bad time."
2. "I help HVAC owners follow up on estimates that didn't get a yes. Quick question: when a homeowner gets a replacement quote and goes quiet, who follows up and how many times?"
3. Listen. If "nobody / the office when they have time" → "How many open quotes do you think are sitting in [Jobber] from the last six months?"
4. Offer: "I'd like to try a 30-day pilot on that backlog. Can I show you in 15 minutes how it works?" Book the meeting.
5. If not interested: thank them, log do-not-call, end the call.

## 5. Discovery questionnaire (15–20 min)
1. Which system holds your estimates (Jobber/HCP/ServiceTitan/paper)?
2. Roughly how many estimates did you send last month? Last 6 months?
3. What % do you think sell? Do you measure it? (get number)
4. Average approved job: repair vs replacement?
5. Who follows up on open estimates? How many touches? Over how many days?
6. Roughly how many estimates over 14 days old are still open?
7. What did a lead cost you last quarter (ads/LSA)? What do you spend per month?
8. Have you tried software/agency for follow-up? What did it cost, what happened?
9. If 1 in 12 old estimates closed, what's that worth?
10. Do you collect consent/opt-outs for texting? Do customers expect calls/emails?
11. Who must approve a $750 pilot? Any commission concerns?
12. What would make this a clear success after 30 days?

## 6. Short demonstration (no software to buy)
Use `estimate-tracker-template.csv`. In the meeting, take **their** exported open-estimate list (with permission) and show on screen: age buckets, total quoted value, and a proposed first-week call list. The demo is their own data. Do **not** show invented case studies. If they won't share data, walk through the template with obviously fake "SAMPLE" rows labeled as such.

## 7. Cadence for each unsold estimate (owner-approved wording)
Day 2 email + call → Day 6 call → Day 12 email with financing/availability note → Day 21 last "should I close this out?" message. Stop at any reply of "stop."
